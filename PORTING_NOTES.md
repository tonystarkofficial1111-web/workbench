### Block World: glue bugs found by driving the original and the port side by side
The engine itself is byte-identical to the original; every difference was in the glue around it:
1. **Squashed picture** - `game:resize` was sent before React had made the Block World section visible, so the renderer sized itself from
   the window instead of the section and the canvas buffer had the wrong aspect. `WorldTab` now sends it in a layout effect after the commit.
2. **Coins never saved** - boss / dawn rewards called the page's `wbSaveProfile()`, which does not exist here; it threw inside a try/catch.
   The converter now routes it to `commitProfile()` (and no longer writes the React-owned coin counter by hand).
3. **Export / Import world ran twice** - the engine attached its own listeners to the User / Saves buttons that React already wires
   (two downloads, the file imported twice, status text written into a React node). The converter drops the engine's copy.
4. **Start > Block World card did nothing** (a quirk of the original: it looked for a tab button that Block World does not have).
   It now opens Block World at its mode picker, like Arcade > Block World.
Everything else was checked with real mouse-style events in both builds (and in the obfuscated build via `build.ts --dbg`): all building and
defense buttons place and charge wood, 1x / 10x speed, Shaders, Follow the King, Instructions, Record clip, Back to Arcade, Resume, the mode
picker, the Workshop / Market / Armory menus, and the classic Save / Load / Command mode / Buildings controls.
Still the original's behaviour: Legacy and the classic sandbox start with Shaders off (Infinite switches them on by itself); the classic
hotbar is dimmed and not clickable in Command mode (`#hotbar.dim{pointer-events:none}`) and only works after switching to Build mode.

## Infinite world overhaul (session 3)
Requested: stunning graphics for the Infinite mode only, with a low setting for Chromebooks; moving water; a map about twice as big (still an island,
now a jungle); sunsets and mornings, realistic weather with wind that moves the flags; more detailed buildings; trees that fall; caves with
paths; zombies that look like zombies and walk differently; armoured, named villagers with a halo in the Armory; a clearer Instructions page.
(The Legacy mode and the classic sandbox must stay as they were.)
- **Where**: `src/world/infinite/*` (see the README table). `engine.ts` is still generated and never edited by hand: `tools/convert-world.ts`
  makes a handful of small patches (camera pitch, zoom limit, tree-fall and ground-pick hooks, the Shaders auto-switch) and hands the engine's
  closure bindings to `installInfinite(env)` as getters / setters (`ENV_READ`, `ENV_BIND`, `ENV_CONST`) and the 5.56 closure's internals as `env.iife`.
- **Rule for every wrapper**: act only when `ctx.isInf()` (the Infinite world is running or starting), otherwise call the original. Checked by driving
  Legacy after Infinite (original castle and boxes, no Infinite state left behind) and Infinite twice in a row (no leaked models or animations).
- **Island**: radius 56 (was 26), grass radius 42. The ground is one flat layer rendered as merged meshes; the engine still sees one tiny `Block`
  per land cell so `getGroundHeight`, the nav grid and placement keep working. Land is y = 1 (camera minimum zoom is 10, so the camera never sinks under it).
  Trees keep the engine's tree contract (invisible pick proxies); chopped trees go through `wbTreeDown` for the fall animation.
- **Nature is blocky, not round** (asked for after the first pass: "a little more blocky, less circular", nature and trees only): trees are square trunks that
  step sideways in whole blocks under leaf clumps made of boxes (a main block plus top, side and corner blocks, the extras only from Medium / High), palms and
  bamboo are stacked square segments, stumps and bushes and loose rocks and cave boulders are blocks, fronds and grass blades are stepped flat pieces (`shapes.ts`),
  trees and plants stand in quarter turns, the ground / sand / path textures are square-cornered, rain splashes are square ripples, and the sky has a square sun and
  moon and pixel-cell clouds. Buildings, people, halos and the UI were left as they were on purpose.
- **Models**: `mb.ts` merges primitives into a few vertex-coloured meshes per model. Buildings are built when `createBuilding` runs (the placement preview
  and the building placed from it get the same variant); the Armory / Market / Workshop menus open from the new models because every model mesh is a
  *direct* child of `building.mesh` (the engine's click test only looks at those). Defenses replace `iife.MESH.*` and keep the contracts the 5.56 code relies
  on (`userData.guard` / `flame` / `halo` / `glow`, the tower deck at y = 4, wall `WL` x `WT`, trap `scale.y`); walls, torches and traps share geometry between copies.
  The castle keeps `castle.pickables`, `hbSprite` and `group`, and is registered as a walking obstacle (r = 3) so zombies queue outside its walls.
- **Moving parts** (`anim.ts`): flags turn to point downwind and wave in the cloth shader, smoke drifts with the wind, weather vanes point into it, windmill sails
  and the cap follow it, lamps (windows, lanterns, forge) are dim by day and bright at night and in storms. Items belong to an owner and are dropped when it leaves the scene.
- **Graphics levels** (`gfx.ts`): tiers, pixel ratio, shadows, water / sky quality, foliage amount, particles. The engine's Shaders button and the Developer-options
  performance monitor are kept in step with it. `applyShadowFlags` is wrapped so ground, plants, water and sky never cast shadows.
- **Not changed on purpose**: docks cannot be built on the Infinite island ("No coastline for a dock!"), exactly as in the original (the coast lies outside the
  territory radius the dock code searches); the original's Instructions text for the other modes; the zombie rules (spawn times, wave sizes, boss every 10th).
- **How it was checked**: headless Chrome over CDP (`tools/cdp.ts`) with the `--dev` build and the test hooks: every build / defense button placed through real mouse-style
  events, the three menus clicked on the new models, Graphics High -> Medium -> Low -> High with triangle counts per model, wind in two directions, sunrise / sunset /
  night, a zombie night against walls, towers, torches and traps, dawn crumbling, Legacy and a second Infinite run.

## F1 Cockpit 5.61.0: driving rules, 3D seat, crash scenes (session 5)
Requested: Forza-like graphics, a more logical game (steering left for long enough leaves the road, a barrier touch disqualifies), 20 random ~7 s crash scenes
with all the specs, an accurate cockpit (no see-through floor), the building that stood on the track fixed, a manual gearbox (Y / U), controls top left / map top right /
a bright red countdown for the corner (only a warning now), a more detailed interior and working suspension.
- **Split of work**: the generated game keeps the race and the telemetry; `src/arcade/f1/sim.ts` has the road rules and is the only place that moves the car across the road.
  The loop calls `f1Sim.physics()` (gearbox, position, suspension, barrier) and `f1Sim.corner()` (warning); `f1Crash()` ends the race and hands over to the view's crash scene.
  Every per-frame step in the loop is multiplied by `dk` (frame time / 16.67 ms), which also fixes the original running faster above 60 Hz (a lap at an emulated 60 Hz
  and at 130 fps both take 12 s).
- **Grip model** (arcade, because a lap here is 500-650 m): `D = |kappa| * v^2` against `G = 120 * tyre grip` (m/s^2, 55% on gravel); beyond G the car gets an outward
  acceleration of `0.6 * (D - G)` (max 120), steering gives a sideways speed of up to 4.6 m/s. That makes the Circle flat out, the Technical Loop need ~60 mph in its
  tightest bend, the Complex ~48 and the Curvy Ring ~33. Gravel drag is `0.04 + 0.006 * mph` per frame (a crawl out is always possible).
- **Corner direction check** (same as in 5.60.0): 30 of 32 TURN corners sit on a bend that turns the same way in 3D; the other two (Curvy Ring) sit on an inflection in the game's own path.
- **Cockpit** (`cockpit.ts`): a second small scene drawn into its own picture and composited after the speed blur (so only the world blurs), with its own near plane. The car
  group is turned by the world camera's orientation (so reflections and the sun are right) and the camera sits at the driver's eyes inside it. The mirrors are real: the view renders
  the scene from behind into two small render targets (Balanced: one per frame at 192 px, High: both at 320 px, Low: a still picture); the shadow map is drawn once per frame and
  shared (`renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`). A multisampled render target only copies its picture out when something is rendered into it: clearing it is not enough
  (this showed the cockpit through the crash scenes until `post.ts` rendered an empty scene after the clear).
- **Crash scenes** (`crash.ts`): the player's car is rebuilt from `buildF1Car` (separate meshes per part) and simulated as rigid spheres with spin, ground and barrier collisions;
  parts that come off become bodies of their own (`scene.attach` keeps their place). Pools of instanced boxes make sparks, smoke, fire, dust and debris (blocky, like the rest);
  `world.breakBarrier()` knocks quads out of the barrier and fence index buffers (`repair()` restores them on restart). Scenes are data (`CRASH_SCENES`: speed, spin, bounce, parts lost,
  fire, camera sequence, slow motion) scaled by the impact speed; the director cuts between 11 camera rigs. A point light is created with the scene (intensity 0) so no shader is rebuilt
  when the first flash happens. Sounds are generated (no files).
- **Buildings on the track**: the pit building and stands were boxes on a straight line, which stood on other stretches of the track on the Technical, Complex and Curvy
  circuits. `world.ts` now samples each footprint against the whole circuit (`path.nearest`) and slides / flips / skips a building that does not fit (top-down views of all four maps checked).
- **How it was checked** (`tools/f1-lab.ts` repeats the lap, race, crash and top-down runs): headless Chrome (`tools/cdp.ts`) with the `--dev` build: an autopilot that holds a target speed, slows for the bends ahead, shifts at the right revs and steers back to the middle
  finished a lap of every circuit and a 5-lap race with a pit stop; a "reckless" driver was disqualified on the Technical Loop; a driver steering into the barrier triggered each of the 20
  scenes (no errors, ~170 fps on the RTX 4070 laptop used); the report, Restart, Graphics cycling, close / reopen, a lost WebGL context and a refused context (2D fallback with the short crash screen) were all exercised.
  Numbers from the suspension over a Technical lap: roll up to 3.3 deg, pitch 2.7 deg, wheel travel +-10 cm, 6.4 g sideways, 2.7 g braking.

## Car games 3D (session 7, version 5.63.0)
Requested: the F1 controls were inverted; two more gears (ten); more detail but still blocky; Drift King and Rally in 3D (Drift King on night docks with a
Skyline or a 2021 Mustang 5.0 and maps of rising difficulty; Rally like the F1 Cockpit but off-road with two rally cars and a choice of map); Take Your Time with
~10 s 3D fast-forward clips like the crash scenes (a mechanic fitting piston rings with the tools and so on). Asked first: steering and touch buttons were
the inverted ones; clips plus 3D drives; four maps each; in parts, one HTML at the end.
- **F1 "inverted"**: the car moved the right way (checked by projecting the road ahead on screen while holding A / D) but the wheel and the front tyres turned the
  opposite way, because the car model faces +z and its right is -x. Fixed in `car.ts` (`root.rotation.y = -steer`) and `cockpit.ts` (`wheelRot.rotation.z = +steer`).
- **Ten gears**: `GEAR_MAX` lives in `src/arcade/f1/consts.ts` and the game's copy is now `F1_GEAR_MAX.slice()` (converter patch); acceleration per gear `0.62 - 0.036 * gear`.
- **Shared 3D layer** (`src/arcade/car3d`): the physics is a bicycle model with the tyre curve `sin(1.4 atan(B a))` (peaks near 12 degrees and settles at ~80%, which
  is what lets a car slide and be held). Two things were needed to make it playable: the driven tyres keep at least ~half their side grip while spinning (otherwise
  full throttle in first gear is an endless donut), and a drift assist that only acts past ~45 degrees of body slip. Particles are cubes in one instanced mesh with a
  per-instance alpha attribute (patched into the shader with `onBeforeCompile`) and they thin out near the camera; rotating transparent cubes read as shards, so
  smoke and dust stay axis-aligned (voxel clouds).
- **Tracks**: both games reuse `TrackPath` from the F1 code with scale 1 (metres). Rally densifies its control points (Catmull-Rom every ~7 m) so a crest can be sharp
  enough to jump; take-off happens in `Vehicle` when the ground falls away faster than gravity. Walls are lateral limits per path sample (Drift King, from the quay edge;
  Rally, the stage edge where trees / rocks stand); cameras are clamped inside them.
- **Bugs found while testing**: the global `button:not(.ghost)` rule beat the menu cards (raised specificity), instanced rally trees floated (the variant was built at
  the origin's ground height), canyon walls stood inside the drivable edge, metal looked black without an environment map (PMREM: room / sky / night).
- **Take Your Time**: the game logic (timing bars, points, stages) is untouched; converter patches route `assemblyVisual`, `animateAssembly` and the shift stages to
  `createThinkers3D`. Clips are functions of time over keyframed tracks; the mechanic's hands, hips and look point are keyed and IK bends the elbows and knees.
- **How it was checked** (headless Chrome, `--dev`): steering projection test; F1 laps on Circle / Technical / Complex; Drift King on all four courses with an autopilot
  (drift chains, wall hits, result screen, profile score, save, graphics switch, closing the overlay cleans up); Rally on all four stages (pace notes, rivals, jumps,
  damage, cockpit / bonnet / chase framing, result, save, close); Take Your Time end to end with every clip filmed at several times, hit and miss variants, both hill stages.
